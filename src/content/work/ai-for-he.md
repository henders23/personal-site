## Context

University staff are being asked to have a position on AI — by students who
already use it, by institutions writing policy, and by tools arriving faster
than committees can meet. Most available training answers the wrong
question: it demonstrates products when staff need *practice*.

I design and teach courses on AI for higher education staff: what these
models actually do, what that means for assessment and academic writing,
and how to design learning experiences with AI in the loop rather than
bolted on.

## The problem, precisely

A workshop that tours ChatGPT features is obsolete in a term and changes no
one's teaching. The durable skills are older than the tools: task design,
critique of generated text, and knowing what you refuse to delegate. The
design problem was to build a course about AI whose core content would
still be true after the next model release.

## Design decisions

**Hands on the tools from minute one.** Every session is built around
participants doing something real to their own materials — redesigning
their own assessment brief, interrogating a model's summary of their own
reading list. No hypothetical examples; the friction is the curriculum.

**Critique is a skill, taught explicitly.** Sessions treat model output the
way EAP treats student drafts: as text to be read closely, marked up, and
questioned about its sources, hedges and omissions. Staff who can mark an
essay already have the muscle; the course points it at a new genre.

**The syllabus names what doesn't change.** Cognitive load, retrieval,
feedback, academic integrity — the course anchors every AI question to
learning principles that will outlive the tools, so the content degrades
slowly by design.

<aside class="note">The Learning Gallery and the EAP coach both feature as

## Where AI fit

Twice over: AI is the subject, and AI helped build the course — generating
the deliberately flawed model outputs used in critique exercises, and
stress-testing activity instructions by role-playing a confused
participant before any human met them.

## What happened

The courses run and rerun; the critique exercises have proved the most
durable component, surviving three generations of model releases without a
rewrite.

## What I'd change

Less time on capability, earlier on refusal: the most valuable session
discussion is always "what should stay human in your teaching?" — and I
now think the course should open there rather than earn its way to it.
